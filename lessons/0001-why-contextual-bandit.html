Lesson 01 · Adaptive Agent Router

Why this project is a contextual bandit

One task arrives. The router sees a little context, chooses one strategy, and learns from the result. That single sentence is the whole RL connection.

Tangible win: after this lesson, you should be able to label every important object in the project as context, action, feedback, or policy—and explain why this is not full RL.

Bandit, not full RL

Bandit

There is one decision for the current task. The task ends after the selected strategy returns and is evaluated.

Contextual

The best strategy can depend on the task. Arithmetic may favor tool; easy extraction may favor direct.

Not full RL

The current answer does not create a next environment state that determines a chain of future decisions.

For the formal distinction, read Chapters 1–2 of Sutton & Barto. For contextual-bandit foundations, use Chapter 18 of Lattimore & Szepesvári.

How the policies differ

  • Epsilon-greedy and UCB learn global action values: “which strategy is good on average?”
  • LinUCB uses the feature vector: “which strategy is good for this kind of task?”
  • The full benchmark runs all strategies for comparison; online replay observes only the chosen strategy.

The LinUCB connection is made formal in Chu et al., “Contextual Bandits with Linear Payoff Functions”.
